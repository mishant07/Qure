The CDC recommends at least one-time hepatitis C screening for all adults,
with periodic testing for people with ongoing risk factors. Age and sex
enter screening models as epidemiological background: prevalence varies
across birth cohorts and exposure patterns, so demography adjusts the prior
probability that an abnormal panel reflects true HCV pathology.

The recommended testing sequence is anti-HCV antibody screening followed by
HCV RNA testing to confirm active infection — antibody positivity alone
cannot distinguish cleared from current infection. A biochemical screening
flag, however strong, never substitutes for this sequence.

For a flagged individual, the practical next steps are: share the screening
summary with a clinician, obtain antibody plus RNA testing, and avoid
alcohol and hepatotoxic exposures in the interim. Modern direct-acting
antiviral regimens cure the great majority of confirmed cases, which is why
catching the signal early matters.
