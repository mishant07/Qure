Beyond injury enzymes, the liver panel measures what the liver produces and
clears. Albumin (ALB) and total protein (PROT) reflect synthetic capacity:
persistently low values suggest chronic impairment rather than a transient
insult. Cholinesterase (CHE), also synthesised hepatically, moves in the
same direction in chronic liver dysfunction.

Bilirubin (BIL) measures clearance. A rising bilirubin with low albumin is
the classic pairing of a liver that is both injured and failing to
compensate, and it features heavily in prognosis-oriented assessment of
chronic hepatitis cohorts.

In screening use, these markers temper enzyme-only readings. Raised ALT/AST
with preserved albumin and normal bilirubin suggests active but compensated
injury; the same enzymes with depressed albumin and raised bilirubin
resemble decompensating patterns and raise follow-up urgency. Distinguishing
the two is a clinician's task, informed by trends rather than single values.
