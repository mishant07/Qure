Liver chemistry interpretation starts with pattern recognition. A
hepatocellular pattern — ALT and AST raised disproportionately — points
toward injury of the liver cells themselves, as seen in viral hepatitis.
A cholestatic pattern — alkaline phosphatase (ALP) and gamma-glutamyl
transferase (GGT) raised disproportionately — points instead toward bile
flow obstruction.

The ALT/AST relationship adds nuance: ALT is the more liver-specific of the
pair, while AST also originates in muscle, heart and red cells. GGT helps
adjudicate an isolated ALP rise, supporting a hepatic rather than bony
source when both move together.

Screening models lean on this joint behaviour. A flag driven by ALT/AST
with quiet ALP/GGT resembles the hepatocellular pattern of hepatitis cohorts;
one driven by ALP/GGT instead suggests a different workup path. Either way,
enzyme patterns localise the question — they do not answer it — and
hepatitis serology plus imaging complete the picture.
