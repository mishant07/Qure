Cholesterol (CHOL) and creatinine (CREA) are not hepatitis markers, and an
honest screening report says so plainly. They travel with liver panels
because chronic liver disease disturbs lipid synthesis and because kidney
function must be known before contrast imaging, antiviral dosing or any
invasive workup.

A low cholesterol in the context of depressed albumin and raised bilirubin
fits the broader picture of failing hepatic synthesis rather than primary
lipid disease. A raised creatinine instead redirects attention toward renal
function or hepatorenal physiology, changing the urgency and the choice of
follow-up tests.

Their value in a screening pattern is therefore contextual: they corroborate
or complicate the hepatic story told by ALT, AST, albumin and bilirubin.
When they dominate a flag without enzyme support, the indicated follow-up
is metabolic and renal review rather than hepatitis serology.
