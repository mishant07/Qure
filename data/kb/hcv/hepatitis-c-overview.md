title: Hepatitis C infection and liver injury

Hepatitis C virus infects liver cells and, in a large fraction of cases,
establishes chronic infection that progresses silently for years. Ongoing
hepatocellular injury leaks intracellular enzymes — alanine transaminase
(ALT) and aspartate transaminase (AST) — into the blood, which is why these
two markers anchor most hepatitis screening patterns.

The World Health Organization notes that most acutely infected people show
no symptoms, so biochemical signals often precede any clinical complaint by
a long interval. A screening flag built on raised transaminases is therefore
best understood as an early biochemical smoke signal, not a diagnosis of
any particular fibrosis stage.

Confirmation always requires virus-specific testing (anti-HCV antibody
followed by HCV RNA), because many non-viral conditions also raise ALT and
AST. The screening result simply prioritises who should receive that
confirmatory workup first.
