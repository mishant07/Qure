Resting blood pressure and serum cholesterol are the two great modifiable
drivers of coronary risk. Sustained hypertension accelerates endothelial
injury and left-ventricular strain, while elevated cholesterol — particularly
LDL fractions — supplies the lipid core of atherosclerotic plaque.

The American Heart Association groups these with smoking, diabetes,
overweight and physical inactivity as the major controllable factors. Unlike
age or sex, they respond to treatment, which is why screening reports
highlight them: they point at actions (measurement confirmation, lifestyle
change, clinician-guided therapy) rather than fixed destiny.

A single resting reading is noisy — blood pressure varies across the day
and cholesterol requires fasting-standardised laboratory measurement. Models
treat them as continuous risk gradients, not thresholds, and confirmatory
measurement is part of any follow-up.

When these features dominate a screening flag, the constructive next step
is a primary-care review of recent readings, medication adherence and
lifestyle factors.
