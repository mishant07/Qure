title: Chest pain types and likelihood of cardiac origin
source: Diamond GA — A clinically relevant classification of chest discomfort (JACC, 1983)

Not all chest discomfort carries the same cardiac weight. The classical
classification distinguishes typical angina (substernal pressure provoked by
exertion or emotion and relieved by rest), atypical angina (meeting two of
the three criteria), non-anginal pain (one or none) and asymptomatic
presentation.

Typical exertional angina has the highest predictive value for obstructive
coronary disease; asymptomatic presentation is the least informative single
finding and must be interpreted together with exercise and ECG features.
This gradient is why chest-pain character is one of the strongest single
contributors in coronary screening models.

An important caveat for screening use: pain description is subjective and
overlaps with oesophageal, musculoskeletal and anxiety-related discomfort.
A model flag driven mainly by chest-pain type is a prompt for objective
testing (exercise ECG, imaging), not a conclusion.

Clinicians combine the pain classification with age, sex, risk factors and
test results before deciding on further evaluation.
