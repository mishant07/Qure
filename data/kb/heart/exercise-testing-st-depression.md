---
title: Exercise testing, ST depression and angina response

Exercise stress testing provokes the supply-demand mismatch of myocardial
ischemia in a controlled setting. ST-segment depression measured during or
after exercise (oldpeak) is a classical marker of ischemia, and its depth
correlates broadly with the extent of coronary disease.

The slope of the ST segment adds information: a flat or downsloping ST
response is more concerning than an upsloping one at comparable depression
depth. Exercise-induced angina (exang) during the test further raises the
likelihood that the ECG changes reflect true ischemia rather than artefact.

Maximum heart rate achieved (thalach) matters for interpretation. Failure to
reach an adequate chronotropic response can render a test non-diagnostic,
while a normal high workload without ECG changes or symptoms lowers the
post-test probability of significant stenosis.

For screening purposes, a cluster of exercise-response flags — deeper ST
depression, flat or downsloping morphology, induced angina — is a standard
trigger for clinician-ordered confirmatory testing such as imaging or
angiography.
