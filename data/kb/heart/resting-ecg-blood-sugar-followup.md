A resting ECG documents baseline electrical abnormalities such as ST-T
changes or left-ventricular hypertrophy patterns. An abnormal resting trace
both adds independent risk information and conditions how exercise ECG
changes are read — some baseline patterns make stress interpretation harder
and push clinicians toward imaging instead.

Fasting blood sugar above the diabetic range marks diabetes, itself a major
coronary risk equivalent in AHA guidance. The heart-diabetes link runs both
ways: diabetes accelerates atherosclerosis, and coronary patients are
routinely screened for undiagnosed dysglycemia.

The number of major vessels showing disease (ca) and thalassemia-region
perfusion defects (thal) come from fluoroscopy and nuclear imaging — tests a
clinician orders after screening, not screening inputs in themselves. In the
model they function as strong confirmatory-pattern features: when they
contribute to a flag, the pattern resembles angiographically confirmed
disease cohorts.

A flag dominated by these features should therefore be read as "resembles
patterns that warranted imaging follow-up in clinical practice" — and the
next step is exactly that follow-up, arranged by a clinician.
