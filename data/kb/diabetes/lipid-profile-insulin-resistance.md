The classic dyslipidemia of insulin resistance pairs high triglycerides (TG)
with low HDL cholesterol, often with raised VLDL carrying the triglyceride
load. Total cholesterol (Chol) and LDL add background cardiovascular context
rather than glycemic signal directly.

This joint movement is why lipid features contribute as a group in diabetes
screening patterns: an isolated borderline triglyceride means little, while
high TG together with depressed HDL and raised VLDL reproduces the textbook
metabolic-syndrome fingerprint that accompanies emerging Type 2 diabetes.

Lipid values respond to fasting state, alcohol, thyroid status and
medication, so screening deviations call for standardised repeat
measurement rather than immediate conclusions. Their constructive role in a
flag is to suggest the metabolic workup — fasting lipids, glucose tolerance
assessment — that a clinician would order next.
