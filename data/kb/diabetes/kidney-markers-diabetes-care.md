Urea and creatinine (Cr) are kidney-function markers, not diabetes
diagnostics, and screening reports should keep that distinction visible.
Their relevance to diabetes is downstream: chronic hyperglycemia injures the
renal microvasculature, so ADA standards build routine kidney surveillance
into diabetes care from diagnosis onward.

Rising creatinine or urea alongside diabetic-range HbA1c fits the
complication-surveillance pattern — established or emerging glycemic burden
with early renal accompaniment — and raises the stakes of follow-up without
changing the classification itself. The same elevations with quiet glycemic
markers instead suggest primary renal, hydration or medication effects and a
different workup.

Either configuration points toward the same next step: clinician-ordered
repeat metabolic panel with estimated glomerular filtration rate and urine
albumin assessment, interpreted against trends rather than single values.
