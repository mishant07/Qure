title: Body-mass index and Type 2 diabetes risk

Body-mass index above the obese range is among the strongest population
predictors of Type 2 diabetes, operating through insulin resistance: excess
adiposity, particularly visceral, blunts insulin signalling long before
glycemia itself rises. The CDC notes that modest sustained weight loss
substantially reduces progression risk in high-risk individuals.

In screening patterns BMI usually plays a supporting role — amplifying the
significance of borderline HbA1c or lipid deviations rather than driving the
verdict alone. A flag where BMI is the leading contributor with otherwise
quiet labs resembles an at-risk-phenotype pattern: risk-factor burden
without yet-manifest dysglycemia.

Because BMI cannot distinguish fat from lean mass and says nothing about
fat distribution, it is a triage signal, not an assessment. Waist
measurement, activity history and family background complete the picture at
follow-up.
