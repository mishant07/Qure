title: Age, sex and who gets screened for diabetes

Type 2 diabetes incidence rises with age as beta-cell reserve declines and
lifetime adiposity exposure accumulates, which is why guidelines converge on
routine screening from middle adulthood onward, earlier where risk factors
cluster. Sex modifies the background rate modestly and shapes how risk
factors distribute, but never determines classification alone.

In screening models, age and sex therefore function as epidemiological
priors: they adjust how surprising a given metabolic pattern is, rather than
supplying metabolic evidence themselves. A lipid-HbA1c pattern carries more
weight in a younger adult precisely because the prior is lower — and the
same pattern in an older adult still requires identical confirmatory steps.

Family history, gestational-diabetes history, hypertension and cardiovascular
disease complete the risk-factor set the CDC lists; the model's demographic
features are a compressed proxy for this background, and the clinician visit
fills in the rest.
